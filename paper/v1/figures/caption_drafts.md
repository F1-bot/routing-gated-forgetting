## fig_arch (new; suggested place: Section 4, paragraph Architectures, `\label{fig:arch}`)

Architectures and adaptation protocol, each drawn for one layer, with frozen parts marked by a snowflake. (a) The dense model applies one attention block and one SwiGLU MLP of width 1,920 to every token; it has 12 layers of width 480 with 16 heads and 0.14B parameters. (b) In the MoE model a frozen router sends each token to 3 of the 24 SwiGLU experts (width 640) of every layer and weights their outputs with renormalized gates $g_m(x)$; recorded at $\theta_0$ over the tokens of a corpus $c$, the gates give every expert the second moment $w_c(m)=\mathrm{E}_c[g_m^2]$, and an expert subset $S$ carries the routing mass $W_c(S)=\sum_{m\in S}w_c(m)$ (the bars are illustrative); the model has 12 layers of width 480 with 16 heads and stores 0.37B parameters. (c) In \nestor{} a frozen column router sends each token, once per block, to $K_c$ of the 8 columns, with $K_c=3, 4, 5, 6, 6, 7, 8$ in blocks 1 to 7 (drawn: one layer of block 2, $K_c=4$); every active column runs its own attention heads (5, 4, 3, 3, 3, 2, 2 heads per column by block) over the tokens routed to it and its own frozen router over 8 private experts (top-2 in block 1, top-1 later), 8 shared experts (top-2) serve every token, and a two-way gate weights shared against column experts; the model has 13 layers in 7 blocks, width 480, and stores 0.38B parameters. (d) Every run starts from a pretrained base $\theta_0$, adapts an expert subset $S$ chosen from the routing record at $\theta_0$ ($A_d$ or $B_d$ for C1 on books, $H_d$ or $L_d$ for C2 on German with SGD or Muon, and all weights except the routers in full runs and in the reproduction) for 96 steps of 262,144 tokens at 4k context, with routers and balancing biases frozen, and measures forgetting $F$ as the rise of held-out cross-entropy on 500 windows of 4,096 tokens of the code, mathematics and DCLM streams; residual and depth-read connections are not drawn.

Notes. The dense parameter count (0.14B), the expert width of 640, the heads per column, the private top-k by block and the eight shared experts with top-2 routing are not yet in Section 4; if wanted there, they fit the paragraph Architectures of `04_design.tex`. The numbers come from `figures/fig_arch_facts.json`, whose entries name their source files (base-run `config.json`, the G1 NESTOR run config that loaded the base checkpoint 859/859, stage-B log headers, paper text).

## fig_edge (new; suggested place: Section 6.1, after the paragraph on the pre-freeze measurements, `\label{fig:edge}`)

Cost of adaptation on one 16\,GB consumer GPU (RTX 4080 SUPER), measured as GPU board energy from a 1 Hz power log; the values are descriptive, come from the training segments, are not the registered measures of the cost block and count board power only, without CPU, memory or power-supply losses. (a) Energy per 96-step full adaptation of 25.2M tokens in gate G1: the 7 MoE runs of base 1 (full adaptation on German or mathematics, all-expert adaptation on German, one DCLM continuation), all without activation checkpointing, took a median of 54.0 Wh and 0.248 h, and the 2 NESTOR full adaptations on German, which fit in memory only with activation checkpointing, took 143.9 Wh and 1.25 h, 2.7 times the energy and about five times the time; the out-of-memory attempts without checkpointing that preceded them (17.8 and 2.2 Wh) are not included. (b) Board power of one run of each kind, the MoE run with the median energy and the NESTOR run of the same job (full adaptation on German, data seed 101): 14.9 min at a mean of 218 W and 88\% utilisation against 79.1 min at 111 W and 43\%, that is 0.51 times the mean power for 5.3 times the duration; dashed lines mark the means, and the low-power opening minutes lie before the first logged training step. (c) Context extension of stage C (48 steps of 1,048,576 tokens, single runs): the dense model and MoE base 1 completed at 8,192 (8k) and 16,384 (16k) tokens, the MoE model only with activation checkpointing after one out-of-memory attempt at each length (5.5 and 3.3 Wh, hatched), whereas all 6 NESTOR attempts, at equal tokens or equal FLOPs and micro-batches 2 and 1 with checkpointing, ran out of memory (17.8 Wh at 8k and 7.7 Wh at 16k without a result); a further probe at 8k with micro-batch 1, activation checkpointing and a memory fraction of 0.93 ran out of memory at the first step, with 14.31\,GiB allocated and 0 bytes free.

Notes. Panel (a) is the median comparison of the paragraph that begins 'Before the freeze' in `06b_edge.tex` (0.248 h, 54.0 Wh, 1.25 h, 143.9 Wh, ratio 2.7); utilization and mean power per run are in the caption only. The memory-fraction 0.93 probe of `05_reproduction.tex` (paragraph Context extension) is not in the energy record and is not drawn; the caption reads its settings and memory state from `data/stageC_results.json` (`nestor.attempts`). The panel titles name the measured quantity, and the two NESTOR bars of panel (c) carry the marker 'exceeds 16 GB'. The traces come from `figures/fig_edge_values.json` (`scripts/fig_edge_extract.py`), which reproduces the record's energy, mean power and utilization of both windows before writing; timestamps of the power log are local time (UTC+2) and are converted as in `edge_pre_freeze.py`.

Domain adaptation of stage B, the analogue of \citep[Table~6]{chu2026nested} at our scale: the dense model, MoE base 1 and \nestor{}, 100M-class models whose bases were pretrained on 0.5B tokens, are each adapted for 96 steps of 262,144 tokens at 4k context on books (PG19), mathematics (OpenWebMath), code (AlgebraicStack) and German (FineWeb2), with routers frozen in the routed models. (a) Forgetting is the increase of cross-entropy on 500 windows of 4,096 tokens of DCLM shard 09 (mean of the four corpora: dense 0.157, MoE 0.107, \nestor{} 0.115 nats); every cell is a single run, and its 95\% window-bootstrap interval (4,000 resamples, window sampling only, no seed replication) lies within the marker. (b) Forgetting relative to MoE, $100(F-F_{\mathrm{MoE}})/F_{\mathrm{MoE}}$, with intervals from the same paired resamples: on the four-corpus mean the dense model forgets +46.4\% [45.2, 47.7] and \nestor{} +7.4\% [6.4, 8.3] relative to MoE, and \nestor{} lies below MoE only on mathematics, at $-$11.7\% [$-$14.8, $-$8.7]. The lower facet shows the means of the preprint's Table 6 (+21.1\% for the dense model and $-$2.7\% for \nestor{}), which come from 500M-scale \nestor{} and MoE models and a 1B dense model adapted for 0.5B tokens at 32k context; they are drawn with open markers in a separate facet because those runs differ from ours in scale, adaptation budget and context and are not peers of our single runs.

## fig_design (crossover tables; replaces the caption of `fig:design` in `01_introduction.tex`)

The two pre-registered crossover designs, drawn for one layer of a 24-expert MoE base; filled squares show one draw, and the signs in each table give how the log forgetting of the runs enters the crossover statistic, so each table reads as a difference of differences. (a) C1, target books: experts are ordered by the tilt of their routing between code and mathematics, and subsets $A_d$ and $B_d$ take 6 of the 12 experts of the lower and the upper pool in every layer, matched on books and DCLM routing mass within 5\% ($\log1.05$). Each subset is adapted alone on books, and its log forgetting on code and on mathematics, summed with the signs shown, gives the crossover $I_d$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:Id}, which is predicted to equal the contrast $P_d$ of routing at the pretrained weights; the test asks whether $J_d=I_d-0.25\,P_d$ is positive on average over the draws. (b) C2, target German: $L_d$ and $H_d$ take 6 of the 8 experts with the lowest and the highest German routing mass in every layer and are matched on DCLM routing mass within 5\%; each is adapted on German twice, with SGD, under which experts that the target uses more move further, and with per-matrix Muon, which takes a step of equal size for every matrix of a shape. Summed with the signs shown, the log DCLM forgetting of the four runs ($F_{\mathrm{SGD}}$ and $F_{\mathrm{Muon}}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:I2}) gives the optimizer interaction $I2_d$, which is predicted to be positive; the test asks whether $J2_d=I2_d-0.25\,\Delta_{Q,d}$ is positive on average at the evaluation step $\sstar$, which gate G2-3 fixed at 8, so C2 is confirmatory.

## fig_gap (revised panel text; caption of `fig:gap` in `05_reproduction.tex`)

Training-loss trajectories of the 0.5B-token reproduction (one logged batch loss every 10 steps; lines are LOESS smooths for display, statistics use the raw points). (a) Training loss of the four runs, with the first 0.09B tokens above the plotted range; both MoE seeds end lowest and \nestor{} ends level with the dense model, and the top axis gives the learning rate relative to its peak, which falls from 0.62 of peak at the start of the last half (shaded) to 0.018 at the last logged step. (b) Gaps between runs, with LOESS bands; diamonds mark the held-out val09 gaps of the final checkpoints, and the MoE seed difference serves as the noise reference. Over the last half the \nestor{}$-$MoE gap of the two-seed mean averages 0.157 nats [0.147, 0.167] and changes by $-$0.006 nats [$-$0.040, +0.031], so no change is detectable.

Notes. The panel titles now name the plotted quantity; the comparisons they stated before are in the caption, (a) as the ordering at the end and (b) as the last-half mean gap, read from `data/gap_trajectory.json` (`gaps.N_minus_Mavg.last_half`).
